What changed in V3, and why

Research made us undo three things from Version 2

The sticker shelf no longer shows empty slots. V2 displayed grey "locked" stickers telling kids what to do to earn them. A meta-analysis of 128 experiments found that kind of promised reward measurably reduces a child's own motivation. Stickers here are souvenirs of what you did — never announced in advance, never counted.

Pressing YET no longer earns anything. The moment encouragement pays out, it becomes a transaction.

"YET!" on its own is not enough. Carol Dweck calls effort-praise without a strategy "false growth mindset" — so YET now names the real thing and hands back one concrete idea to try.

💪

The Grit Zone — nothing to get wrong

There is no right answer and no buzzer. Every choice shows what might happen next, and invites another try. Answers carry a picture so a four-year-old can read them.

☕ The grown-up half

Every kid activity ships with its matching talk-guide — the pattern the strongest education sites use. This one would say:

"Ask your child: what fell down for you this week — and what different way could you try?"

Notice the praise never says "you're so smart." It names what the child did.

The Power of YET — now it does real work

Pick the thing that's hard right now. Gritty says it back to you — and gives you one idea to try. He never promises you'll get it. He just knows "not yet" isn't "not ever."

I can't ride my bike… YET!

🎁

Gritty's Gifts — souvenirs, not prizes

No points, no timers, nothing to lose, and no empty slots showing what you haven't done. Stickers appear when you try things — they're a memory of where you've been, not a score.

Your stickers live inside this computer, in this browser. Nobody else can see them — not even us. If you use a different computer, or clear your browser, they'll be gone. That's okay.

Why this site is quiet during stories

A claim Kim can make that animated competitors can't

A 2025 meta-analysis of 22 studies and nearly 2,000 children found that tap-to-play features in digital picture books did nothing for story comprehension — and mini-games actively hurt it. An earlier review of 43 studies found the same split, and that the harm fell hardest on children with less support at home.

So: nothing to tap while a story is running. All the play lives in the Grit Zone, after the story, where interrupting costs nothing. Kim can say this on her About page in her own voice as a teacher — and teachers will forward it.

"There's no hurry. Gritty will be here."
